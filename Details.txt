1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RESEARCH PROBLEM FRAMING
====================================================================================================
Last-mile delivery operations are increasingly evaluated by customer-facing and carrier-dispatch
service thresholds. While machine learning delay models can predict stop-level delivery lateness
prior to route departure, predicting risk alone does not prescribe operational routing actions. High-risk
stops often occupy spatial or topological positions where forward relocation would introduce severe route
detours or disrupt downstream stops.

This study develops the Prediction-Informed Selective Route Resequencing (PISR) framework for the
Service-Threshold Delivery Lateness Mitigation Problem (STDLMP, legacy acronym PTDLMP). Rather than
triggering costly whole-route re-optimization or fleet reassignment, PISR pairs calibrated ex-ante
lateness risk (p_i) with an ex-ante intervention actionability metric (g_i = max Delta TT) under bounded
intervention budgets (B) and cumulative route-distance tolerances (delta).

Key Scientific Findings:
1. Actionability Dominance: Policies that incorporate spatial-temporal actionability (RA: p_i * g_i,
   AB: g_i) achieve massive tardiness reductions (>1,400 minutes, ~12.1% reduction) with minimal distance
   expansion (+2.05%), outperforming pure risk-based targeting (RB: +367.63 min, 2.9% reduction) and
   conventional slack/deadline heuristics by more than 3x.
2. RA vs. AB Equivalence & Nuanced Scientific Framing: RA does not outperform AB on average (-16.62 min
   difference favoring AB, p = 0.4825). The rigorous scientific conclusion is:
   "Actionability drives the operational gains; risk-only targeting performs poorly; and an incremental
   benefit from multiplying actionability by predicted risk has not been demonstrated in this cohort."
3. Statistical Multiplying Correction: Evaluated across 48 scenario cells (3 test routes x 4 budgets x 4
   tolerances). At the cell level, RA/AB significantly outperform RB, Slack, Deadline, and Random (p < 0.0001).
   Under route-clustered testing (N=3 independent routes) with Holm-Bonferroni correction, none of the
   comparisons achieves formal significance (p_Holm = 0.0631 for RA vs. RB). The large point differences
   represent exploratory empirical evidence rather than statistical confirmation. An exploratory planning
   estimate of N approx 34 independent routes is required to detect subtle differentials.
4. Mathematical Monotonicity Guarantee: Proposition 1 guarantees that total route tardiness never increases
   (Delta TT >= 0 in 100% of 288 benchmark runs).
5. Computational Viability: Actionability precomputation averages 1.31 s per route; SFR resequencing
   averages 0.26 s per route; total decision-to-execution latency averages ~1.57 s, confirming viability
   for morning dispatch staging.
6. Explicit-Deadline Audit: In the official Amazon 13-route cohort, only 128 of 2,038 drop-off stops (6.3%)
   have explicit time windows (`time_window.end_time_utc`), with 0 late stops under baseline dispatch in the
   held-out test split. The primary operational study therefore evaluates a derived 4-hour dispatch service
   threshold (tau_i = departure + 4.0h), audited across H in {3, 4, 5, 6} hours.

2.1 NOTATION TABLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

R^0             Ex-ante baseline customer service sequence (e.g., Nearest Neighbor or Clarke-Wright)
R'              Modified customer service sequence after selective forward relocation
tau_i           Dispatch service threshold (deadline) for customer i: tau_i(H) = t_departure + H
s_i             Service / dwell duration at customer stop i (seconds)
t(u, v)         Travel time from stop u to stop v (seconds), queried from official travel-time matrix
d(u, v)         Straight-line Haversine distance from stop u to stop v (km)

T_i(R)          Tardiness of customer i under route R: T_i(R) = max(0, c_i(R) - tau_i) in minutes
TT(R)           Total route tardiness: TT(R) = sum_{i in C} T_i(R)
NL(R)           Number of late deliveries: NL(R) = sum_{i in C} 1(c_i(R) > tau_i)
D(R)            Total route distance: D(R) = sum_{k=0}^{n-1} d(sigma(k), sigma(k+1))
Delta TT        Reduction in total route tardiness: Delta TT = TT(R^0) - TT(R')
Delta NL        Reduction in late delivery count: Delta NL = NL(R^0) - NL(R')
Delta D         Fractional increase in route distance: Delta D = (D(R') - D(R^0)) / D(R^0)
B               Intervention budget: maximum fraction of stops relocated, B in {5%, 10%, 20%, 30%}
k_max           Maximum allowed moves: k_max = floor(B * n)
delta           Cumulative route-distance tolerance: delta in {0%, 2%, 5%, 10%}
p_i             Calibrated predicted probability that customer i violates service threshold under R^0
g_i             Intervention actionability score of customer i
Delta TT_ij     Potential reduction in total tardiness if customer i is relocated forward to position j
Delta D_ij      Distance increment resulting from forward relocating customer i to position j

2.2 SCHEDULE PROPAGATION & METRICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Given an open route sequence R = [sigma(0), sigma(1), ..., sigma(n)] departing depot sigma(0) at
timestamp t_departure:
  Arrival timestamp at stop sigma(k):
    a_{sigma(k)}(R) = c_{sigma(k-1)}(R) + t(sigma(k-1), sigma(k))
  Completion timestamp at stop sigma(k):
    c_{sigma(k)}(R) = a_{sigma(k)}(R) + s_{sigma(k)}
  Stop-level tardiness:
    T_{sigma(k)}(R) = max(0, (c_{sigma(k)}(R) - tau_{sigma(k)}) / 60.0)  [minutes]
  Cumulative metrics:
    TT(R) = sum_{k=1}^n T_{sigma(k)}(R)
    NL(R) = sum_{k=1}^n 1(c_{sigma(k)}(R) > tau_{sigma(k)})
    D(R)  = sum_{k=0}^{n-1} d(sigma(k), sigma(k+1))

2.3 ACTIONABILITY DEFINITION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For customer i at initial position u in sequence R^0, forward relocation tests inserting customer i
into each upstream position j < u. The actionability score g_i is defined as the maximum feasible
tardiness reduction achievable by any single forward move of customer i:
  g_i = max_{j < u} { Delta TT_{ij} : Delta D_{ij} <= delta * D(R^0) and Delta TT_{ij} >= 0 }
If no forward insertion satisfies feasibility, g_i = 0.0.

Targeting Policies:
  1. Risk-Actionability (RA, Proposed):    Score_i = p_i * g_i
  2. Actionability-Based (AB, Comparator):  Score_i = g_i
  3. Risk-Based (RB, Comparator):           Score_i = p_i
  4. Slack-Based:                           Score_i = -(tau_i - c_i(R^0))
  5. Operational Threshold / Deadline:      Score_i = -tau_i
  6. Uniform Random Baseline:               Score_i ~ Uniform(0, 1)

2.4 LOCAL EDGE RELOCATION & DISTANCE DELTA
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When moving customer i from downstream position u to upstream position j (j < u):
Let u_prev = sigma(u-1), u_next = sigma(u+1) (or None if u == n).
Let j_prev = sigma(j-1), j_curr = sigma(j).

Removed edges:
  1. (j_prev, j_curr)
  2. (u_prev, i)
  3. (i, u_next)  [if u_next exists]
Added edges:
  1. (j_prev, i)
  2. (i, j_curr)
  3. (u_prev, u_next)  [if u_next exists]

Exact O(1) Local Distance Delta:
  If u == n (open route termination, customer i was final stop):
    Delta D_local = d(j_prev, i) + d(i, j_curr) - d(j_prev, j_curr) - d(u_prev, i)
  If u < n:
    Delta D_local = d(j_prev, i) + d(i, j_curr) + d(u_prev, u_next)
                    - d(j_prev, j_curr) - d(u_prev, i) - d(i, u_next)

Mathematical Verification: Verified via property-based testing across 10,000 random moves that
  | Delta D_local - (D(R_{insert}) - D(R)) | < 10^{-9} km.

2.5 SEQUENTIAL FORWARD RELOCATION (SFR) ALGORITHM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input:  Baseline route R^0, candidate priority scores {Score_i}, budget B, distance tolerance delta
Output: Monotonically improved feasible route R'

1: R_curr <- R^0
2: k_max <- floor(B * |C|)
3: moves_accepted <- 0
4: Sort customer candidates C in descending order of Score_i -> CandidateQueue
5: for each candidate customer i in CandidateQueue do
6:    if moves_accepted >= k_max then break
7:    u <- current position of customer i in R_curr
8:    best_j <- None; best_gain <- 0.0; best_cost <- infinity
9:    for j = 1 to u - 1 do
10:      Delta D_ij <- ComputeLocalDistanceDelta(R_curr, u, j)
11:      if D(R_curr) + Delta D_ij > (1 + delta) * D(R^0) then continue
12:      R_cand <- SimulateInsert(R_curr, u, j)
13:      Delta TT_ij <- TT(R_curr) - TT(R_cand)
14:      if Delta TT_ij > best_gain or (Delta TT_ij == best_gain and Delta D_ij < best_cost) then
15:         if Delta TT_ij > 0 then
16:            best_gain <- Delta TT_ij
17:            best_cost <- Delta D_ij
18:            best_j <- j
19:   if best_j is not None then
20:      R_curr <- SimulateInsert(R_curr, u, best_j)
21:      moves_accepted <- moves_accepted + 1
22: return R_curr

2.6 PROPOSITION 1: SCHEDULE MONOTONICITY GUARANTEE & PROOF
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Proposition 1 (Schedule Monotonicity).
Let R^0 be an ex-ante baseline delivery route. Let R' be the route produced by the Sequential
Forward Relocation (SFR) algorithm under acceptance condition Delta TT_{ij} > 0.
Then:
  (i)   TT(R') <= TT(R^0) (total route tardiness never increases).
  (ii)  D(R') <= (1 + delta) * D(R^0) (cumulative distance tolerance is strictly satisfied).
  (iii) Invariant: min_{runs} Delta TT >= 0.

Formal Mathematical Proof:
Proof of Part (i):
SFR initializes R^(0) = R^0. At each step m in {1, ..., k_max}, a relocation is accepted if and only if
there exists an insertion position j < u such that Delta TT = TT(R^(m-1)) - TT(R^(cand)) > 0.
If such a position exists, R^(m) = R^(cand), which implies TT(R^(m)) < TT(R^(m-1)).
If no such position exists, the candidate is skipped and R^(m) = R^(m-1), implying TT(R^(m)) = TT(R^(m-1)).
By finite induction over all m <= k_max:
  TT(R') = TT(R^(k_max)) <= TT(R^(k_max-1)) <= ... <= TT(R^(0)) = TT(R^0).
Therefore, TT(R^0) - TT(R') = Delta TT >= 0. Q.E.D.

Proof of Part (ii):
At every candidate evaluation, line 11 enforces:
  D(R_curr) + Delta D_{ij} <= (1 + delta) * D(R^0).
Because initial route R^0 trivially satisfies D(R^0) <= (1 + delta) * D(R^0), and every state
transition explicitly checks that the post-move cumulative distance remains within (1 + delta) * D(R^0),
the final route R' strictly satisfies D(R') <= (1 + delta) * D(R^0). Q.E.D.

Empirical Verification: Verified across 288 benchmark runs, 480 random seed runs, and 72 Clarke-Wright
runs (total 840 route executions): zero monotonicity violations (min Delta TT = 0.0000 min) and zero
distance violations.

3.3 MODULE 3: DISTANCE & LOCAL EDGE DELTA ENGINE (`code/src/routing/distance.py`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implements spherical Haversine distance and O(1) local edge delta computation:
```python
def haversine_distance(lat1: float, lon1: float, lat2: float, lon2: float) -> float:
    R = 6371.0088  # Earth radius in km
    phi1, phi2 = math.radians(lat1), math.radians(lat2)
    dphi = math.radians(lat2 - lat1)
    dlambda = math.radians(lon2 - lon1)
    a = math.sin(dphi / 2.0)**2 + math.cos(phi1) * math.cos(phi2) * math.sin(dlambda / 2.0)**2
    return 2.0 * R * math.atan2(math.sqrt(a), math.sqrt(1.0 - a))

def compute_local_distance_delta(route: List[str], instance: RouteInstance,
                                 from_idx: int, to_idx: int) -> float:
    # O(1) exact distance delta when relocating customer from from_idx to to_idx (to_idx < from_idx)
    u, j = from_idx, to_idx
    u_node = route[u]
    u_prev = route[u - 1]
    u_next = route[u + 1] if u + 1 < len(route) else None
    j_node = route[j]
    j_prev = route[j - 1]

    # Added edges: (j_prev -> u_node), (u_node -> j_node)
    dist_added = instance.get_distance(j_prev, u_node) + instance.get_distance(u_node, j_node)
    dist_removed = instance.get_distance(j_prev, j_node) + instance.get_distance(u_prev, u_node)

    if u_next is not None:
        dist_added += instance.get_distance(u_prev, u_next)
        dist_removed += instance.get_distance(u_node, u_next)

    return dist_added - dist_removed
```

3.4 MODULE 4: SCHEDULE PROPAGATION ENGINE (`code/src/routing/schedule.py`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Propagates route sequences chronologically through planned service times and official travel times:
```python
def propagate_schedule(route: List[str], instance: RouteInstance) -> ScheduleResult:
    curr_time = instance.departure_time
    arrival_times = {instance.depot_id: curr_time}
    completion_times = {instance.depot_id: curr_time}
    tardiness = {}
    lateness = {}
    total_dist = 0.0

    for idx in range(1, len(route)):
        prev_stop = route[idx - 1]
        curr_stop = route[idx]
        travel_sec = instance.get_travel_time(prev_stop, curr_stop)
        total_dist += instance.get_distance(prev_stop, curr_stop)
        curr_time = curr_time + timedelta(seconds=travel_sec)
        arrival_times[curr_stop] = curr_time
        stop_obj = instance.stops[curr_stop]
        curr_time = curr_time + timedelta(seconds=stop_obj.service_seconds)
        completion_times[curr_stop] = curr_time

        if stop_obj.promised_time is not None:
            delay_sec = (curr_time - stop_obj.promised_time).total_seconds()
            tard_min = max(0.0, delay_sec / 60.0)
            tardiness[curr_stop] = tard_min
            lateness[curr_stop] = 1 if delay_sec > 0 else 0
        else:
            tardiness[curr_stop] = 0.0
            lateness[curr_stop] = 0

    return ScheduleResult(
        route=route, arrival_times=arrival_times, completion_times=completion_times,
        lateness=lateness, tardiness_minutes=tardiness,
        total_lateness=sum(lateness.values()), total_tardiness=sum(tardiness.values()),
        total_distance=total_dist
    )
```

3.5 MODULE 5: ACTIONABILITY KERNEL (`code/src/routing/actionability.py`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evaluates the maximum potential tardiness reduction g_i for each customer by evaluating forward moves:
```python
def compute_actionability_scores(instance: RouteInstance, base_route: List[str],
                                 delta: float = 0.05) -> Dict[str, float]:
    base_sched = propagate_schedule(base_route, instance)
    base_tt = base_sched.total_tardiness
    base_dist = base_sched.total_distance
    max_allowed_dist = base_dist * (1.0 + delta)
    g_scores = {}

    for u in range(1, len(base_route)):
        cust_id = base_route[u]
        best_delta_tt = 0.0
        for j in range(1, u):
            d_delta = compute_local_distance_delta(base_route, instance, u, j)
            if base_dist + d_delta > max_allowed_dist:
                continue
            cand_route = base_route[:j] + [cust_id] + base_route[j:u] + base_route[u+1:]
            cand_sched = propagate_schedule(cand_route, instance)
            gain = base_tt - cand_sched.total_tardiness
            if gain > best_delta_tt:
                best_delta_tt = gain
        g_scores[cust_id] = best_delta_tt
    return g_scores
```

```python
def compute_holm_bonferroni_p_values(p_values: Dict[str, float]) -> Dict[str, float]:
    # Sort comparisons in ascending order of unadjusted p-value
    sorted_items = sorted(p_values.items(), key=lambda x: x[1])
    m = len(sorted_items)
    adjusted_p = {}
    running_max = 0.0
    for rank, (name, p_val) in enumerate(sorted_items):
        k = rank + 1
        adj_p = min(1.0, (m - k + 1) * p_val)
        running_max = max(running_max, adj_p)
        adjusted_p[name] = min(1.0, running_max)
    return adjusted_p
```

4.2 RQ1 MACHINE LEARNING PREDICTION RESULTS

7.3 PRIOR THESIS CODE PROVENANCE & CLEAN-ROOM IMPLEMENTATION AUDIT
